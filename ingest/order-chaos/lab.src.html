Which states looked fragile before they broke?

I scored 682 states from 3400 BCE to 2024 on one question: will this state break in the next 50 years? Each score comes from its territory alone, and each one is tested on states the model never saw. The answer splits cleanly in two.

States that withered (lost half their peak land first)76%

were flagged as fragile 5 to 40 years before they ended. Slow deaths leave a trail on the map.

States that snapped (ended near their peak)18%

were flagged. Conquest, civil war or sudden partition came out of a clear sky, at least as far as borders can see.

      The scoreboard

      Out of sample · polity-years every 10 years

      The model is a plain logistic regression on six readings: size, share of peak territory, change over 25 and 100 years, age, and era. States are split in two by name; each half is scored by a model fitted on the other half. A harder test fits on years before 1500 and scores everything after.

      Does a 30% score mean 30%?

      Each dot is a tenth of all scores. On the line means the score is honest.

      Against the baseline

      The baseline knows only a state's age and era. Territory adds real signal, though not much, and it weakens on the later centuries.

        Peter Turchin argues that order breaks from the inside on a schedule. Population grows until land runs short, wages fall, elites multiply and fight for posts, and the treasury drains. Then comes the crisis. This toy version of his demographic-fiscal model lets you pull the levers. It is not fitted to any real state.
